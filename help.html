Using Trindle, and why it works that way

What each part does and how to use it, with the reason it was made that way. Nothing here needs the terminal unless it says so.

Safety

Important buttons (Power, Permissions, installing in Apps, save and open windows, screen sharing, the saved-logins box) only answer your own presses.

Why: a program pretending to be you cannot switch things off, install things or take your files or screen. (15 Oct 2026)

Flow, from Trindle's own catalogue, counts as your own hands.

Why: you use it to work this computer from another one. (15 Oct 2026)

An access tool you switch on yourself in Settings (clicking when the pointer rests, or a single switch) counts as your own hand.

Why: people who click that way must still be able to use every part of Trindle. (15 Oct 2026)

If something other than you presses a power button in the ring, the label under the ring says "Only you can shut this computer down."

Why: you can see why nothing happened. (15 Oct 2026)

Whenever Trindle refuses something or closes a page by itself, it says why in a label right where you are working.

Why: nothing should happen without you knowing the reason. (15 Oct 2026)

Older programs and games that did not come from Trindle's own catalogue each run in a private space of their own, only while open, and Settings has a switch to turn that off.

Why: they cannot then read each other's keys and windows. (15 Oct 2026)

Wallpaper

Wallpapers can be up to 4K (3840 by 2160), 20 MB for a still picture and 200 MB for a moving one. Bigger files are left out the moment you pick them, and a label says why; a picture bigger than your screen, or a moving one, gets a short note about memory.

Why: a huge wallpaper slows the whole computer for no gain. (15 Oct 2026)

Apps

"Install all" in Apps never installs a plugin update that asks for a new permission; it waits for you with a label saying so.

Why: nothing gets new powers in a batch you did not look at. (15 Oct 2026)

The ring

If the ring stops working, a label with a small alert sign appears in the middle of the screen where the ring would be: "The ring stopped. Press Super to restart it."

Why: you are never left wondering where the ring went. (15 Oct 2026)

The eye-dropper, for picking any colour on screen, has its own button in the middle of the ring beside Screenshot.

Why: it is used often enough to be one press away. (15 Oct 2026)

Screenshots

If the screenshot tool freezes, it is closed for you, a label says so, and a Try again button starts it afresh.

Why: nothing frozen is left sitting over your screen. (15 Oct 2026)

Folders

A login kept for a shared folder is remembered for 90 days and starts again each time you use it; the switch says so.

Why: a login should not be kept for ever without you knowing. (15 Oct 2026)

Saving a file back to another computer writes a fresh copy first and swaps it in only when complete.

Why: a dropped connection never leaves you with half a file. (15 Oct 2026)

Paste in Folders moves only what you cut in Folders; files cut in another program are copied and the original stays.

Why: no other program can make Folders take your files away. (15 Oct 2026)

Folders tells its areas apart: the side bar is a step lighter than the page, the foot is a strip of its own, and one quiet line runs under the top bar.

Why: the whole window was very dark and the areas blended into each other; a step of grey and one fine line separate them without shouting. (18 Oct 2026)

While a big copy or move runs, a quiet line in its row says "Copying uses the disk heavily, so other programs may feel slower until it ends."

Why: Folders copies at the lowest priority, but some drives (most NVMe ones) ignore priorities, so other programs can still wait on the disk; progress is also shown only four times a second and folders are listed once per burst, to keep Folders itself quick. (18 Oct 2026)

What is being copied or moved is greyed and cannot be moved or copied again until it finishes ("Already being copied or moved."), and what is arriving is shown greyed under a spare name until it is whole.

Why: so a second try never offers to replace the thing that is still arriving, and a transfer that fails leaves nothing behind. (18 Oct 2026)

Notifications

Notifications are circles in a corner you choose; Close, Snooze (10 minutes, 1 hour, 6 hours, 8 the next morning) and Clear all sit along the bottom; drag a circle anywhere to keep it as a reminder, and Clear all leaves it alone.

Why: an important message can wait where you will see it. (15 Oct 2026)

Keyboard and mouse

Any key or mouse button can be changed in Settings, Keyboard and mouse: into another key or button, keys held together, a run of keys, nothing, or one thing tapped and another held. Ready-made swaps such as Ctrl and Caps Lock are one switch each. Changes work everywhere, from the login screen on.

Why: nobody should need a separate remapping program, and one change should work in every program and game. (15 Oct 2026)

Ctrl, Alt and an F key always reach a text console, whatever you change, and trindle-settings --keys default there puts every key back.

Why: no change can lock you out of your own keyboard. (15 Oct 2026)

Keyboards and mice that a program makes (remapping tools, game launchers) still type and play, but cannot press protected buttons or type into password questions unless you switch "Counts as you" on for them by name in Settings, Keyboard and mouse.

Why: a program pretending to be your keyboard cannot switch things off, install things or answer a question meant for you. (15 Oct 2026)

Trindle does not yet have layers (one key switching every other key to a second set).

Why: said plainly so nobody hunts for it. (15 Oct 2026)

Game controllers get a profile each on the same Settings page: swap A and B, turn a stick round, set a dead zone, or make a button a key for a game with no controller support. A game's own controller layer should be switched off for a controller changed here.

Why: one place changes every controller for every game, and a second layer would change it twice. (15 Oct 2026)

Under the screen lock and at the login screen, Backspace, Tab, Enter, Escape and Shift are never changed and no key becomes nothing, though your swaps still hold.

Why: no change can leave your password impossible to type. (17 Oct 2026)

Drives

Drives can make room for a new partition while you work, on Btrfs only (the system's own disk, encrypted or not): after the administrator's password it takes a snapshot first, then makes the files, then the encrypted copy, then the part smaller, checking the sizes after each step, and leaves free space for you to make the new part in. It asks for the password twice (the snapshot, then the change), and anything doubtful is refused with its reason. Other kinds of disk say in a label why they cannot be made smaller while in use, because they can only shrink when nothing is using them.

Why: the order means a stop part-way never leaves anything sticking out of what holds it, and there is no need to start from a USB stick. (17 Oct 2026)

Trindle

No bar, no dock, no menu. A desktop with just one ring, written in Rust and made for a mouse or a touchscreen.
